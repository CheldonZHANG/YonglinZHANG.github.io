My research connects mechanics, reaction and transport, and materials processing. I use experiments and numerical models to identify the physical mechanisms that control structure formation in microfluidic systems.

## Interfacial polymerization

At HKUST, I study the multiscale evolution of reaction–diffusion and phase separation during interfacial polymerization. This work addresses the formation of functional membranes and microcapsules and examines how water and kinetic gating affect interfacial instability and morphology. Related papers appeared in [*ACS Catalysis*](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.5c08183) and [*Soft Matter*](https://doi.org/10.1039/d6sm00601a), both with equal first-author contributions.

## Microfluidic interconnection

My work on electroless copper interconnection addresses transport and uniformity in three-dimensional chip integration. I investigated the role of fluid flow and reaction kinetics in bubble and microvoid formation, developed a coupled phase-field model for reactive flow, and derived a dimensionless scaling law for deposition uniformity and void formation regimes. Related first-author papers appeared in [*International Communications in Heat and Mass Transfer*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icheatmasstransfer.2025.110454) and [*Chemical Engineering Journal*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cej.2022.134684).

## Mechanics and adaptive materials

My earlier research at Harbin Institute of Technology covered shape memory polymer composites, thermomechanical behavior, and adaptive structures, including grippers and deployable systems. I have also worked on electronic package reliability under thermal, mechanical, and electrical loading.
